# NPC and location authoring: researched workflow

## Match scope before adding a situation

The user's main plot is theirs to write. For ordinary towns, buildings and incidental people, fill local blanks with a useful role, modest character and everyday details. Do not automatically add a shared issue, pressure, secret, deadline, conspiracy or story hook. A genre/theme, bundle request or existing town name does not grant permission for an overarching arc.

Town overview: named buildings/functions, perhaps one relevant person per building, and a broad layout. A specific-building request zooms into its proprietor, a few staff/regulars, compact spaces and services. Names plus one playable trait are usually enough; omit unnecessary Skill blocks. Local family/work/neighborhood relationships are welcome, but should not grow into imposed plots.

Only when the user requests a developed situation, adventure or contextual integration, choose an issue/resource, interested actors, obstruction and potential changes without intervention; derive agendas, information and hooks from those commitments. Randomize presentation afterward. Vary the conflict rather than making every character a benevolent technician blocked by bureaucracy.

For existing campaigns reuse named actors and established facts. Treat generated material as proposals. A locked name alone does not make a generated job/location match the user's intended fiction; review semantic constraints explicitly. A user note is not automatic natural-language constraint enforcement.

## Developed NPC checklist — not a minimum for incidental NPCs

The checklist below is for developed/major characters. For Goons use only one Aspect, one Skill, 0–1 Stress boxes and a short behaviour cue; for everyday NPCs one Aspect, relevant Skills and a practical Hook are enough. Respect the main skill's 40/65-word budgets. Do not require every low-tier person to have an agenda/offer/boundary/secret/relationship/backstory section. Promote or expand only when play or the user calls for it.

- Identity/role and one visible first impression.
- One concrete immediate want, with an obstacle. Prefer a verb plus an object over a noun such as greed.
- Something they can offer, a principled boundary or pressure point, and a named relationship.
- One delivery cue a GM can perform without adopting an accent or caricature.
- Useful knowledge separated from biography. A secret is an established fact, several plausible discovery channels, and why learning it changes decisions.
- An exit/concession option where conflict is possible. Allies, bystanders and opponents can all have agendas; tier is not morality.

## Location detail follows zoom

- **Town overview:** brief identity; named buildings and their functions; optional one local face per building; simple layout if useful. No default main Hook or crisis.
- **Specific building:** proprietor and a few staff/regulars, each with one concise cue; compact spaces and ordinary services. One or two sensory/useful details or a fitting Aspect are sufficient. Local relatives or neighboring businesses can connect naturally without carrying a story arc.
- **Active scene, dangerous site or requested adventure context:** add interactive features, relevant pressure, telegraphed danger/counterplay, routes/zones, opportunities and discoveries only as needed. These are not mandatory fields for a saloon or barber shop.
- Do not manufacture secrets, factions, deadlines or required clues merely because a location template contains such headings. Stop where the user's requested level stops.

## Fate aspects and attributes

Load fate-generation-presets.md. Keep Core/Condensed skills, FAE approaches, descriptive mook competence bands and optional attribute hacks distinct. Do not invent Strength/Dexterity as a second standard Fate layer.

Use clear, specific aspect phrases that create fictional permissions, benefits and meaningful complications. For important aspects explain potential useful and complicating applications; aim for two of each when reviewing major characters, but do not turn this advice into a mandatory toll for simple scene aspects. Do not attach automatic free +2s. Separate invoke examples, hostile invokes and negotiated compels. Stunts specify competency/action/circumstance/effect and applicable limits; label proposals for table review.

Use only role-appropriate mechanics, explicit stress capacities and marking policy, and empty consequence slots. No default HP conversion, assumed private NPC fate-point purse, or balance guarantee without table context. Locations default to statless places; fractal mechanics are an explicit optional authoring choice, currently unsupported by the offline engine.

## Respect player agency and visibility

Hooks expose choices, stakes and consequences, not a scripted sequence. Avoid writing that players must agree, follow one lead, or suffer one inevitable outcome. Provide redundant discovery opportunities for necessary conclusions; don't force three clues onto every incidental NPC card. Preserve already established discovery facts rather than moving evidence retroactively.

Keep public first impressions apart from GM objectives, secrets, truth statuses, hidden mechanics and tactics. Public exports use an allowlist. A raw source backup is GM-only even when the selected projected card is public. Manually pasted secrets cannot be automatically recognized by semantic labels; review before sharing. Legacy generic card fields have no trusted visibility metadata.

## Quality review

Check identity/readability, agenda/stakes, aspect playability, rules correctness, player choice/counterplay, connected fiction, variation and compactness. Reject broken links, contradictions, unsupported rules mixes or ignored constraints. Prefer actionable depth to lots of labels. Distinguish technical schema validation from narrative quality: many passing seed tests do not prove prose is interesting.

Avoid identity-to-morality associations, disability as menace, poverty/foreignness as danger shorthand, attractiveness as personality, or ethnic-accent requirements. Use original content, not copied proprietary random tables.
